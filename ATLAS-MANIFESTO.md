# Atlas Manifesto

We believe tabletop roleplaying is about shared imagination, not software.

Technology should disappear into the background, allowing players to focus on story, characters, and meaningful choices.

Atlas is designed to become an intelligent participant in every campaign: not replacing the Game Master, but amplifying creativity, preserving continuity, and reducing repetitive work.

Every decision made in Atlas should strengthen immersion, consistency, and player agency.

Player agency always overrides AI convenience.

Artificial intelligence may propose, narrate, react, and remember. It may summarize history, surface continuity risks, suggest pacing, and reduce repetitive work. It must never replace player choice.

Campaigns should remember.

NPCs should remember.

Worlds should evolve.

Players should feel that their decisions matter long after a session ends.

Atlas is built for creators as much as players.

Every rule system, setting, voice, campaign, map, and AI personality should be replaceable and extensible without changing the core platform.

The platform belongs to its users.

Campaigns should remain portable.

Offline play should always be possible.

Artificial intelligence should enhance imagination rather than dictate it.

Atlas is not built to automate storytelling.

Atlas is built to make storytelling richer.

Success is not measured by feature count.

Success is measured by memorable adventures.

## How This Manifesto Is Used

This manifesto is the project's identity document. When a product or architecture decision is difficult, contributors should use it as the first test:

- Does the decision improve immersion, consistency, or player agency?
- Does it preserve user ownership?
- Does it keep Atlas extensible?
- Does it make storytelling richer?
